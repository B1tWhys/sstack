# The GUI draws a bar of its own atop the Session's pane

The GUI shows the Session on screen under a bar of its own, drawn by iced like the rest of the window, rather than in the native menu bar of the operating system. Its Session menu starts Sessions, renames and archives the Session on screen, and moves it to another Connection. The bar belongs to the Session's pane, not to the window, because panes are meant to become modes, as in Blender, each with its own bar and its own actions.

iced has no menu bar of its own. We rejected a native one through the `muda` crate for three reasons. A native menu bar belongs to the whole window, so it cannot offer one pane's actions beside another's. On Linux, `muda` needs a GTK window, which winit does not create. And `iced_test` cannot click a native menu, so the agent could not test it headlessly, which ADR 0009 counts on. A bar drawn in the pane works the same on macOS and Linux and is tested like any other widget. The cost is that on macOS nothing appears in the menu at the top of the screen.

## Consequences

Actions that belong to the Session as a whole move into the bar, starting with Archive, which left the row of buttons under the prompt. Send, Interrupt and Rebuild stay beside the prompt. An open menu hangs from the bar over the transcript, a click on the transcript outside it closes it, and while it is open keys go to it instead of to nvim.

iced keeps what each widget remembers, such as how far the transcript is scrolled, the layout of its text and which field has the keyboard, by the widget's place and kind in the tree that `view` returns. It has no other way to tell that a widget is the same one. The first menu wrapped the whole window in a `Stack` only while it was open. That changed the kind of widget at the root, so opening the menu threw away everything every widget remembered: the transcript jumped to the top and was laid out again from nothing, which took long enough to notice. So whatever comes and goes in the window, such as an open menu, the pill over the transcript or the line saying a Rebuild is under way, keeps a place of its own that is always there, empty while nothing is shown. Tests in `window/tests.rs` draw the window frame after frame, carrying over what widgets remember as the runtime does, and check that opening the menu keeps the transcript's scroll and that starting a Rebuild keeps the prompt's keyboard.
